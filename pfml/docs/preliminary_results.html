1Question

Nine states and the District of Columbia now pay wage replacement to parents of newborns and to workers caring for family members, with programmes that differ in duration (4 to 20 weeks), replacement rates (50 to 95 percent) and job protection. Does paid leave raise mothers' labour force attachment, on which margin, for whom, and do the second-generation programmes have larger effects than California's? The evidence rests mostly on California and New Jersey and disagrees: employment continuity around births in the short run, lower employment and earnings for first-time mothers six to ten years later. A design that uses every cohort with one estimator, keeps cohorts separate, and measures the response month by month is the contribution.

2Identification

Treatment is the availability of benefits in the mother's state, timed to the month benefits became payable. With staggered adoption and dynamic effects, two-way fixed effects use already-treated states as controls for later cohorts and are biased. We estimate group-time average treatment effects

ATT(g, t) = E[Yt − Yg−1 | G = g] − E[Yt − Yg−1 | C]

with C the never-treated states (main) or the not-yet-treated states (robustness), aggregated to event time, cohort and calendar time with cohort-size weights. Estimation is doubly robust (csdid, method(drimp)) with age, marital status, race, ethnicity and education, on CPS repeated cross sections, with a wild bootstrap clustered by state. Because state shocks hit all women, the same event studies for childless women are the placebo and the triple difference (mothers of under-6s minus childless women) is the fallback specification.

8Reading

  • Mothers of young children: positive, imprecise. About +3 to +8 points on employment and +2 hours, with cells of 60–150 mothers. Nothing is distinguishable from zero.
  • The placebo fails. Childless women in the two adopting states show lower employment than in control states after adoption and a significant pre-period movement for Connecticut. Paid leave should not move childless women, so these are pandemic-recovery state shocks. The implied triple difference is about +0.10 at event time 1 and equally noisy.
  • What the window shows. The pipeline runs end to end, the aggregations behave as expected, and a within-state comparison group is necessary for this policy. What it cannot show is the effect of paid leave: three pandemic years, two cohorts, no monthly timing. On the IPUMS-CPS monthly files the minimum detectable effect on participation falls from about 10 points to below 1.

9Stata pipeline

00_master.doPaths, logging, package list, runs everything; falls back to the preliminary demo if the IPUMS extract is absent.
01_build_cps.doIPUMS-CPS basic monthly 2000–2024 to the estimation file: outcomes, family structure, cohort variables at monthly and yearly precision, state-cell panels.
02_csdid.doMain estimates: csdid with never-treated controls, drimp, covariates, wild cluster bootstrap; estat simple / group / calendar / event / pretrend; yearly and monthly cohorts; cohort-generation heterogeneity.
03_robustness.doNot-yet-treated controls, triple difference, alternative cohort timing, childless placebo, Borusyak–Jaravel–Spiess, Sun–Abraham, de Chaisemartin–D'Haultfoeuille, HonestDiD sensitivity, TWFE for reference.
04_tables.doesttab tables for the paper.
90_prelim_csdid.doRuns the preliminary specification with csdid on the shipped .dta; should reproduce Table 2.

The do-files are untested: the build environment has no Stata. Two known checks on first run are listed in pfml/stata/README.md.
